# Second-Level Cycle Context

Use this method when the frozen evidence can support a view of consensus expectations, risk
tolerance, capital availability, or the market's position within a non-mechanical cycle.

1. Write the surface interpretation, the range of plausible outcomes, and their uncertainty.
2. Identify what the available price and cited consensus or positioning evidence already imply.
   State precisely how the proposed view differs and what must be true for that difference to
   matter to price.
3. Trace any cycle claim through observable financing, issuance, leverage, risk appetite, inventory,
   or forced-flow evidence. A label such as bull, bear, fear, or greed is not a cycle mechanism.
4. Test both overreaction and justified repricing. List the evidence that would show the apparent
   extreme is not extreme.
5. Prefer risk control or abstention when consensus, price, or the claimed cycle link is missing.

Do not impersonate Howard Marks or any investor. Do not assume cycles are periodic, that an extreme
must immediately reverse, that contrarian action is inherently superior, or that macro timing is
reliable.
