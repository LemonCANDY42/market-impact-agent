# Research Discipline and Contamination Check

Use this general method before forming a market thesis. It counters predictable research and
evaluation bias without suggesting a target, direction, horizon, or preferred conclusion.

1. Inventory the eligible targets and evidence before selecting a thesis. Do not narrow the
   search to familiar issuers, large caps, English-language coverage, or names already associated
   with the event narrative unless the frozen universe requires it.
2. Separate economic role from theme labels. Require an evidenced revenue, cost, capacity,
   funding, policy, risk, attention, or positioning link for every target considered.
3. Check each material source version and availability time. Recent prominence, a later revision,
   or a remembered historical outcome is not point-in-time evidence.
4. State the initial thesis only after the evidence inventory, then deliberately identify the
   strongest disconfirming item, alternative explanation, and missing observation.
5. Treat aliases in a historical benchmark as the complete identity. Do not infer or search for
   the original event, date, issuer, instrument, or realized outcome.
6. Never use an outcome-derived lesson unless it appears in a Pattern Pack frozen before the case
   cutoff. Cross-case memory, debate, and post-outcome reflection are unavailable in holdouts.
7. Preserve an honest abstention when coverage, identity, transmission, or timing is insufficient.
