# Narrative Diffusion Assessment

Use this method only when the frozen evidence includes a timestamped, source-linked narrative
corpus rather than isolated unsourced sentiment.

1. Separate verified event facts from the story used to interpret them.
2. Define each competing narrative, its first visible source, repetition across independent sources,
   audience reach evidence, and the action or expectation it is claimed to affect.
3. Distinguish corroboration of facts from diffusion of a story. Repetition, engagement, or virality
   does not make a claim true or causal.
4. For diffuse or contested cases, preserve multiple explanations and lower conclusion strength.
   Record what observation would discriminate among them.
5. Connect a narrative to price only through an evidence-linked attention, expectation, funding, or
   demand channel. Otherwise abstain.

Do not impersonate Robert Shiller or any investor. Do not use narrative popularity as a trade signal,
infer exact timing from diffusion, or replace the event mechanism with a persuasive story.
