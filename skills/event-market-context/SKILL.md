# Event and Market Context

Use this general method when a public-market event must be distinguished from the market's
prior expectations and surrounding regime. It is adapted from common news, market, and macro
analyst responsibilities without retaining analyst personas.

1. Identify the event's revelation mode and current evidence stage.
2. State the cited prior expectation or baseline and the observed delta. If no defensible
   baseline exists, mark the expectation delta unknown rather than substituting sentiment.
3. Distinguish the physical or corporate fact from the market's reaction to that fact.
4. Use benchmark moves, prices, volatility, flows, technical indicators, prediction markets,
   or macro conditions only when they are present in the frozen evidence. Never invent or
   retrieve an unstated current value.
5. Test whether the information may already be priced, reversed, delayed, or overwhelmed by
   a broader market regime. Absence of market-context evidence is a limitation, not proof that
   the event is unpriced.
6. Carry unresolved context into counterevidence, invalidation conditions, or abstention.
