# liquidity-and-forced-selling

Experimental research method; frozen research cutoff: 2021-09-07.

Evidence status: Case-informed hypothesis; two different mechanisms, not repeated proof of one profitable entry rule.

Question: Is a price decline an adverse information signal, a temporary need to sell, or both?
Evidence: observable redemptions/margin pressure, common ownership where available, contemporaneous volume/spreads, financing conditions, issuer news and executable liquidity. A falling price alone does not establish forced selling.
Steps: compare information-driven and flow-driven explanations; identify who must transact and whether their need can persist; assess correlated exposures across holdings; distinguish an observed trade/quote from a daily aggregate. State which diagnostic evidence is missing.
Action conditions: retaining or adding a long position can be reasonable when the issuer case remains credible, flow pressure has plausible limits and the account can tolerate further loss. Reducing is reasonable when liquidity or fundamentals invalidate the position. Avoid turning a suspected forced sale into an automatic dip purchase.
Failure conditions: hidden solvency damage, continued withdrawals, concentration across apparently different securities, suspended trading, or a rebound too rapid for the permitted execution cadence.
Exit and re-entry: reconsider when cash-flow facts change, financing normalizes, observable pressure abates or losses exceed the original risk assessment. Specify the next observable check; recovery does not require the exact former price.
Supporting case: August 2007 quant deleveraging and withdrawal of market-making capital help explain a sharp dislocation and subsequent recovery. Counter-case: simulated liquidity provision lost money during the stressful week despite profitability before and after. May 6, 2010 exhibited sharp dislocation and rapid recovery; a daily account could not assume access to the intraday recovery.
Transfer limits: leveraged US long-short funds and US ETF microstructure differ from unlevered A-share long-only accounts with price limits and T+1. These episodes do not identify the next trough.
