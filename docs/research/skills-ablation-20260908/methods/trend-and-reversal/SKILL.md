# trend-and-reversal

Experimental research method; frozen research cutoff: 2021-09-07.

Evidence status: Conditional hypothesis informed by long-short momentum evidence; no calibrated long-only rule.

Question: Do prior returns support continuation, or is the position vulnerable to reversal and range-bound turnover?
Evidence: consistently adjusted historical returns at several horizons, raw executable prices, volatility, breadth, liquidity and current event evidence. Distinguish a security's own trend from ranking winners against losers.
Steps: state the observed trend and measurement horizon; test multiple plausible explanations rather than inventing a catalyst; compare continuation with rebound and choppy-market scenarios. Assess whether the account is concentrated in the same trend through several names. Estimate whether additional turnover can plausibly overcome costs.
Action conditions: maintain or add long exposure when trend and available evidence support a forward case at tolerable risk; reduce when reversal evidence or concentration changes the balance. A missing event can coexist with a price-based hypothesis, explicitly labeled as such.
Failure conditions: repeated reversals, late entry, transaction costs, overnight gaps, or sharp rebounds after panic. A shorter lookback is not automatically more responsive in a useful way.
Exit and re-entry: define evidence that would weaken continuation and the next review point. If reducing, retain a concrete re-entry condition based on changed evidence or improved trend; do not require certainty or recovery to the sale price.
Supporting/counter evidence: momentum research documents both persistence and crashes, with prior losers rebounding strongly after stress and hurting short-loser portfolios. This explains a vulnerability of that portfolio construction; it does not prove that a long-only account lost for the same reason.
Transfer limits: short selling, leverage and portfolio construction are central to the cited payoff. No moving-average length, stop threshold or A-share re-entry parameter is validated by this card.

Sources (publication dates, not retrieval dates):
