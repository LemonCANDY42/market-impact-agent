# portfolio-risk-and-reentry

Experimental research method; frozen research cutoff: 2021-09-07.

Evidence status: Conflicting empirical evidence; risk-scaling effectiveness is unresolved for this account.

Question: How much long exposure is justified given the account's positions, common drivers, cash and forward risks?
Evidence: authoritative cash/positions/NAV, current marks and fees; concentration and common economic drivers; trailing volatility/drawdown computed only from available data; a forward investment case and alternative cash/broad-index outcomes.
Steps: separate risk capacity from conviction. Compare retaining exposure, partial reduction and cash. Examine which risks diversification actually changes; several highly correlated names can be one economic bet. Include forgone upside and re-entry friction, not only lower drawdown. Use any scaling estimate available at the time; never infer position weights from the completed sample.
Action conditions: adjust exposure when the forward payoff and account risk justify the change after costs. A 100% single position is allowed by this experiment but still needs a reasoned account assessment. Lower volatility alone does not prove an improved investment outcome.
Failure conditions: unstable volatility/return relationships, fast rebounds after reducing, excessive turnover, crisis correlation increases or an ex post optimizer presented as an implementable policy.
Exit and re-entry: state what changed from the prior decision and what new price/volatility/fundamental evidence would support adding back. Set a review point so that a temporary risk reduction does not become unexplained permanent exit.
Supporting evidence: Moreira and Muir report historical benefits from volatility management. Counterevidence: Cederburg and colleagues examine 103 equity strategies and find no systematic direct outperformance, with practical out-of-sample combinations undermined by instability. Neither establishes this account's optimal exposure.
Transfer limits: some research portfolios allow leverage or short factor legs. This account cannot borrow or short; scaling cannot silently introduce either. No numerical risk threshold is prescribed by the card.

Sources (publication dates, not retrieval dates):
- 2016-06: [Moreira and Muir: Volatility Managed Portfolios](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208).
- 2020-05-01: [Cederburg et al.: On the performance of volatility-managed portfolios](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf).
