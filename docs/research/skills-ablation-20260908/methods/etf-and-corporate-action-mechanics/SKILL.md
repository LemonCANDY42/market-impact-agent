# etf-and-corporate-action-mechanics

Experimental research method; frozen research cutoff: 2021-09-07.

Evidence status: Mechanism and case evidence; corporate-action event alpha remains a gap.

Question: Does the instrument deliver the intended exposure, and can the apparent price/flow effect actually be captured?
Evidence: historically applicable fund mandate/asset class and holdings where available; trading currency, premium/discount with timestamped NAV when meaningful; distributions, splits and entitlement/payment dates; suspension and execution rules; actual liquidity evidence.
Steps: distinguish equity, bond, commodity, cross-border and leveraged/inverse products; look through overlapping holdings; separate total-return adjustment from cash payment and executable raw prices. An adjustment factor is not a cash dividend receipt. Distinguish secondary-market turnover from creations/redemptions and net underlying demand.
Action conditions: select the product whose exposure, costs and available trading conditions fit the account thesis. Compare the broad fund with cash and direct holdings. Do not buy a dividend, split or premium solely because of its headline.
Failure conditions: stale NAV, different trading hours, creation/redemption constraints, liquidity withdrawal, unverified classifications or missing corporate-action entitlement. Unknown valuation or recovery proceeds remain explicit gaps and cannot be set to zero or carried as normal.
Exit and re-entry: reassess exposure mismatch, tracking/price dislocation or suspension; reconsider when reliable prices and trading access return. The hard execution policy remains binding.
Supporting evidence: ETF arbitrage links holdings and fund trading. Counter-case: the May 6, 2010 dislocation included severe ETF price moves despite the diversification label; later price recovery does not prove a feasible fill during the event.
Transfer limits: US ETF evidence does not grant Chinese ETF trading eligibility. Historical Chinese product type, T+1 rules, fees and distributions require their own source evidence. No company-action arbitrage or event-return rule is validated here.

Sources (publication dates, not retrieval dates):

- 2010-09-30: [Joint CFTC-SEC report on the May 6, 2010 market events](https://www.sec.gov/news/studies/2010/marketevents-report.pdf).
