# policy-transmission

Experimental research method; frozen research cutoff: 2021-09-07.

Evidence status: Conditional analytical framework; fiscal and credit-cycle validation remains a gap.

Question: Does a policy announcement change expected cash flows, discount rates, financing access, or only confirm expectations?
Evidence: announcement publication and effective dates; a prior expectation or explicit unknown; contemporaneous rates, equities and credit; target-specific funding and demand exposure.
Steps: separate announced action from its surprise; split discount-rate and economic-information explanations; trace each to the actual security; compare the base case with the opposite interpretation. A rate cut can coexist with bad economic news. Rates and equities rising together is information-consistent, not conclusive identification.
Action conditions: add or maintain long exposure when the incremental transmission and price paid justify it against cash, with material uncertainty stated. Reduce when financing/demand deterioration weakens the original case. No source requires permanent cash or proof of future profit.
Failure conditions: announcement was anticipated; high-frequency co-movement is absent or confounded; policy has delayed or weak pass-through; balance-sheet damage dominates cheaper funding.
Exit and re-entry: name observable financing, orders, or earnings evidence that would reverse the assessment and a review date. After reducing, reconsider on new information rather than requiring a return to the old price.
Supporting evidence: the ECB study distinguishes tightening shocks from positive outlook information in policy announcements. Counter-case: the same rate increase can accompany an equity rise when information dominates; a mechanical rate-to-equity sign rule fails.
Transfer limits: US/euro-area identification and market instruments differ from Chinese administrative credit policy. Fiscal multipliers, Chinese policy-event returns and a robust re-entry rule are not validated here.

Sources (publication dates, not retrieval dates):
